# LogProof: Research Paper Review and Jury Preparation

## Executive summary

The papers support the need for careful log preparation, but they do **not** establish that a universal, lossless, vendor-neutral preprocessing product is new. The closest overlap is Qin et al. (2025): they systematically improve general preprocessing for log parsers. LogProof should therefore distinguish itself by making the *assurance workflow* central: retain received bytes, show field-level source mappings, expose parse quality and structural drift, and replay a candidate firewall parser before approval. That is a product direction and a bounded prototype, not a claim of a complete universal framework. The project README documents both implemented behavior and explicit limits. ([LogProof README](../../README.md))

## Shared ground and meaningful difference

Across the set, preprocessing is a consequential stage: raw logs are heterogeneous or noisy, fields need interpretation, and downstream decisions can be distorted by missing, wrongly parsed, over-filtered or misordered records. Several papers also show that preprocessing is commonly source- or task-specific. ([Qin et al., supplied PDF pp. 1–6](https://doi.org/10.1109/SANER64311.2025.00036); [Zheng et al., pp. 1–3](https://doi.org/10.1109/DSN.2009.5270289); [Hussain et al., pp. 1–3](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICIET.2010.5625730); [Dhanalakshmi et al., pp. 1–5](https://doi.org/10.1109/IACC.2016.35); [Marin-Castro & Tello-Leal, pp. 1–4](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/11/22/10556).)

The key difference is the **question each body of work answers**. Qin optimizes log-template parsing; Zheng preserves failure precursors for prediction; the web papers prepare access logs for usage mining or intrusion detection; the MDPI review concerns business-process event logs. LogProof’s current prototype combines representative format parsing with an evidence-oriented workflow. According to its README, it retains ingested raw records and hashes, presents field mappings, supports selected JSON/CSV/LEEF/Syslog/CEF/NGINX/Windows XML examples, and provides drift/replay/approval/rollback for a firewall parser. It does not implement every vendor format, a complete OCSF mapping, native collectors, SIEM export, or production-scale processing. ([README: formats](../../README.md#formats-in-the-demo); [README: limitations](../../README.md#prototype-boundaries))

**Defensible positioning:** “LogProof is a prototype for verifying how selected security-log parsers transform events, while keeping the received event available for inspection and testing parser changes before promotion.” Do not claim novelty for normalization, general preprocessing, parser drift as a broad research area, or lossless event processing as a field-wide first. State that SHA-256 checks the received/stored bytes; by itself it does not authenticate the sender or prove the pre-ingestion origin of an event. ([README](../../README.md); Zheng et al., supplied PDF pp. 1–3; Qin et al., pp. 1–2.)

## Jury questions to prepare for

1. **“How is this different from Qin et al. or existing parsers?”** Acknowledge their direct work on generalizable variable preprocessing. Show LogProof’s raw-to-field evidence view and the implemented firewall candidate replay gate. Say plainly that broad source coverage and an assurance layer are project goals; do not claim every parser is replay-governed today. ([Qin et al., pp. 1–2, 7–10](https://doi.org/10.1109/SANER64311.2025.00036); [README: prototype boundaries](../../README.md#prototype-boundaries))
2. **“Does a hash prove the event came from a ministry device or was never altered?”** No. It lets this prototype compare stored bytes with the recorded digest after ingestion. Sender authentication, signed receipts, key custody, trusted time and an independently protected audit trail are additional controls not demonstrated by a hash alone.
3. **“Is normalization lossless?”** Normalization can omit or reinterpret source structure. The prototype’s claim is that it retains raw event bytes alongside a limited normalized record and field mappings. Demonstrate a round trip and hash check; distinguish preservation of the raw input from losslessness of the normalized schema. ([README](../../README.md#formats-in-the-demo))
4. **“Is this a universal schema or OCSF implementation?”** No. The README explicitly describes a limited LogProof schema, not complete OCSF conformance. Explain the current fields, unsupported structures, and the path to schema mapping. ([README: prototype boundaries](../../README.md#prototype-boundaries))
5. **“Can this handle billions of events/day or run ministry-wide today?”** Not proven. The documented measurement is a bounded, sequential local microbenchmark, not a distributed/concurrent capacity test. Acknowledge that production deployment needs representative load, storage/retention, backpressure, HA, access controls and security validation. ([README: verification](../../README.md#verify-the-demo); Zheng et al., pp. 1–2, 6.)
6. **“How will you show parser quality and prevent a bad change?”** Present labeled per-source fixtures and field-level precision/recall, parse/validation failure and quarantine counts, unknown-field coverage, drift false-positive/negative rates, replay regressions, and latency. The prototype replay gate currently applies to the firewall example; extending equivalent gates to other source families is future work. ([README: prototype boundaries](../../README.md#prototype-boundaries))
7. **“Could cleaning hide a real attack or rare failure?”** Yes. Zheng et al. show why redundancy reduction can destroy failure patterns; Marin-Castro and Tello-Leal note that rare events can be legitimate. LogProof should keep raw evidence and make any future filtering visible, reversible and measured against known edge cases. ([Zheng et al., pp. 1–4](https://doi.org/10.1109/DSN.2009.5270289); [Marin-Castro & Tello-Leal, pp. 2, 17–24](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/11/22/10556))
